Muscle growth compared

← PT App Tools

Any tool can build muscle

Your muscle cannot tell a barbell from a machine. It responds to how hard it works.

A 2023 analysis that pooled studies comparing free weights with machines found no difference in muscle growth. Strength gains favoured whichever kind people trained with, so a powerlifter still needs the barbell.

Stretch

Load the muscle when it is long

Exercises that challenge a muscle in its stretched position tend to grow it more.

In a 12-week study, overhead triceps extensions grew the triceps 19.9%, against 13.9% for pushdowns with the same sets. In another, seated leg curls, which stretch the hamstrings more, grew them 14% against 9% for lying curls.

Stability

Stable beats wobbly

If your balance or lower back gives out first, the target muscle stops before it has worked hard.

Stable exercises such as machines, cables and chest-supported rows let you take the muscle itself close to failure. That is why the program builder favours them.

Coverage

Two angles for big muscles

Muscles do not always grow evenly along their length. Different exercises put more work on different parts.

For large muscles, two exercises with different angles cover more of the muscle than one. An incline and a flat press for chest, or a squat and a leg extension for quads, are typical pairs.

Fit

Pick what suits your body

The best version is one you feel in the target muscle, that your joints are happy with, and that you can do the same way every rep.

If an exercise hurts a joint, swap it. In the app, tap Edit exercise to see alternatives that train the same muscle.

Consistency

Keep it long enough to progress

Changing exercises every week makes progress impossible to measure, and each new movement costs a few sessions of learning before you can push it hard.

Keep your main exercises for a full training block of several weeks. Change them when they stall for weeks or start to bother your joints.
