Day after a session

Growth happens between sessions

A workout does not make you bigger on the day. It sends the signal. The building happens over the next day or two.

Right after a hard session your muscles are tired and a little weaker. Over the following days they repair and add protein, and if you recover well you come back slightly ahead of where you started.

Frequency

Train each muscle twice a week

Muscle building stays raised for roughly a day and a half after a hard session, then drops back to normal.

When the total weekly sets are the same, studies find only small differences between training a muscle once, twice or three times a week. Twice a week mainly helps by splitting the sets, so each one is done fresh instead of tired.

Soreness

Soreness is not the signal

Next-day soreness comes mostly from new or unfamiliar work. It fades within a few sessions as your body gets used to an exercise, while growth keeps adding up.

A session that leaves you not sore can still be a good one. Still being very sore when the next session comes round is a reason to do a little less, not a sign of extra growth.

Sleep

Sleep is when you build

In one study of 13 young adults, a single night without sleep cut muscle building the next day by about 18%. Cortisol, a stress hormone, rose 21% and testosterone fell 24%.

Most adults need 7–9 hours. Regular sleep and wake times, a dark and cool room, and no caffeine late in the day all help.

Fatigue

Fatigue builds quietly

Hard weeks stack up. Joints ache, sleep gets worse, weights feel heavier, and progress stalls even though you train as hard as ever.

That is built-up fatigue hiding the fitness you have gained. Training harder at this point usually makes it worse.

Deload

Then take a lighter week

A deload is a planned easy week: about half your usual sets, similar weights, and stopping further from failure.

Fatigue fades much faster than muscle, so you come back fresher, often stronger, without losing size. The app suggests one every 6–8 weeks, and sooner if a lift has stalled for 3–4 weeks.
