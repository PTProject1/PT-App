Energy in against energy out

← PT App Energy

Feeding muscle growth

Training sends the signal to grow. Food supplies the energy and the material to act on it.

Start with energy. Your body spends a fairly steady amount each day, mostly on staying alive, then on movement, digestion and training. That total is your maintenance.

Eat below it and new muscle becomes an expense the body can postpone. Eat a little above it and the signal from training has what it needs.

Surplus

A small surplus is enough

Muscle can only be built so fast. Past a modest surplus the extra energy has nowhere useful to go, and it is stored as fat.

Sports nutrition reviews suggest roughly 10–20% above maintenance for newer lifters, which is about 250–500 kcal a day for most people. Advanced lifters gain more slowly and need less.

Protein

Protein has a ceiling

Protein supplies the amino acids that become new muscle. A pooled analysis of 49 training studies found that gains rise with intake up to about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day.

Beyond roughly 2.2 g/kg there is no clear extra benefit. The 0.8 g/kg minimum set for adults who do not train is well short of what supports growth.

Meals

Spread it over the day

Each protein-rich meal raises muscle building for a few hours. Three to six meals of about 0.4 g/kg each is a simple way to reach the daily total.

Exact timing around the workout matters far less than the daily amount. One of those meals within a few hours of training is enough.

Fuel

Then fill in the fuel

With calories and protein set, the rest is fuel. Keep fat at about 0.5–1.5 g/kg to support hormones and vitamin absorption.

Give what is left to carbohydrate, which refills muscle glycogen and powers hard sets. People differ in the split they train best on. Total calories and protein matter most.

Your goal

The numbers move with you

More body fat than you want: a moderate deficit, losing 0.5–1% of body weight a week, with protein toward the top of the range to protect muscle.

New to lifting, or returning: you can gain muscle at around maintenance.

Lean and trained: a small surplus is the fastest route. Bigger bodies need more of everything, which is why the targets are set per kilogram.

Adjust

Let the scale steer

Every calorie target is an estimate. Weigh yourself a few mornings a week and follow the weekly average, because single days swing with water and food.

When gaining, about 0.25–0.5% of body weight a week is a reasonable pace. If the average stays flat for two or three weeks, add 100–200 kcal a day. If it climbs faster, take the same amount away.

Maintenance uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and an activity factor. It can be off by a few hundred kcal for any one person, so treat it as a first guess and adjust from your weekly weight average.

If you carry a lot of body fat

Per-kilogram targets overshoot. Use the weight you are aiming for when you enter your weight for protein.

Plant-based eating

It works. Aim toward the top of the protein range and mix sources, since single plant proteins are a little less efficient gram for gram.

Supplements

Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g a day, has the strongest evidence. Protein powder is only a convenient way to reach your total.
